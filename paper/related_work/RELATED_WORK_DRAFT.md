# Related Work Draft

## Agent Security Monitoring and Oversight

Tool-using language-model agents create safety questions that are not captured by output-only evaluation. ToolEmu evaluates agent failures in an LM-emulated sandbox and applies an automatic evaluator to complete agent traces [@ruan2024toolemu]. Agent Security Bench broadens attack and defense evaluation across scenarios, agents, tools, and model backbones [@zhang2025asb]. These efforts establish that agent risk is an interaction-level property, but their principal endpoints are risky outcomes, attack success, or safety judgments rather than the transferability of a learned normal-trajectory representation.

Monitoring and enforcement work provides a second line of comparison. AI Control studies protocols that use trusted monitoring and limited human labor to prevent intentionally subversive code from being accepted [@greenblatt2024aicontrol]. Task Shield checks whether instructions and tool calls contribute to the user's task and frames indirect-prompt-injection defense as task alignment [@jia2025taskshield]. AgentSpec and Progent instead express explicit runtime constraints or privilege policies over agent actions [@wang2026agentspec; @shi2025progent]. Such approaches can offer semantics that anomaly monitors lack, but require rules, policies, task-alignment judgments, or explicit risk targets. DelegationBench v1 studies a different question: when a monitor learns normal evolution from one execution paradigm, how does benign path variation and a shift in agent action space affect monitoring in another paradigm? PIDR-v1 does not replace runtime policy enforcement; it is a representation-level study under a fixed diagnostic monitor.

Agent safety benchmarks also highlight the importance of dynamic interaction. R-Judge evaluates agent risk awareness, while ToolEmu, AgentDojo, InjecAgent, and ASB execute or emulate tool interactions. The closest work therefore already recognizes that isolated actions omit context. Our distinction is not simply “trajectory rather than action.” It is the paired delayed-exposure design and the cross-paradigm normality-transfer question. We found limited primary work that transfers one trajectory-security monitor between Coding and Web action spaces while separately measuring benign pre-exposure variation. This statement describes the retained literature corpus, not a claim that no such work exists.

## Environmental and Indirect Prompt Injection

Indirect prompt injection exploits the ambiguity between instructions and data in LLM-integrated applications. Greshake et al. demonstrated that adversarial text placed in retrieved content can redirect applications and trigger downstream API use [@greshake2023indirect]. Tensor Trust assembled a large human-generated corpus of prompt-injection attacks and defenses, primarily for prompt hijacking and extraction [@toyer2024tensortrust]. Liu et al. formalized prompt injection and benchmarked attacks and defenses across models and tasks [@liu2024promptinjection].

Tool-agent benchmarks make the threat operational. InjecAgent evaluates indirect prompt injections embedded in content consumed by tool-integrated agents and measures harmful action or data-exfiltration success [@zhan2024injecagent]. AgentDojo provides dynamic tasks in which agents operate tools over untrusted data and evaluates both task utility and security properties [@debenedetti2024agentdojo]. ASB covers prompt injection alongside other attacks and defenses [@zhang2025asb]. Task Shield and IPIGuard represent complementary defense directions: checking task alignment and constraining execution through tool dependency structure.

DelegationBench v1 uses environmental interventions but deliberately does not label every treatment as malicious, unsafe, or an authorization violation. Its outcome is operational trajectory divergence. The intervention is delayed until steps 3, 4, or 5, and control and treatment share an audited pre-exposure generation protocol. This supports comparison of pre- and post-exposure behavior without claiming that divergence equals attack success. Existing injection benchmarks are essential threat-model precedents, but their dominant questions concern exposure-to-harm or defense success. Our benchmark instead measures how intervention-associated variation emerges against benign stochastic path variation and whether its representation transfers across agent paradigms.

## Trajectory Anomaly Detection

The project baselines draw from established anomaly-detection families. Frequency and likelihood methods assign high scores to rare events; first-order Markov models assign high scores to improbable local transitions. The anomaly-detection taxonomy of Chandola et al. distinguishes point, contextual, and collective anomalies and motivates treating full sequences differently from isolated events [@chandola2009survey]. DeepLog learns normal next-event patterns from system-log sequences using an LSTM [@du2017deeplog], while LogAnomaly combines sequential and quantitative signals in unstructured logs [@meng2019loganomaly]. Autoencoder anomaly detection uses reconstruction error as a normality score [@sakurada2014autoencoder]. Distance-based outlier detection based on the kth nearest neighbor is a canonical nonparametric alternative [@ramaswamy2000knn].

Our action-frequency, small sequence-autoencoder, and first-order Markov implementations should therefore be described as project-specific adaptations of canonical families, not direct reproductions of the cited systems. The 5NN transition monitor is likewise an adaptation of distance-to-normal outlier scoring. Nearest-normal transition retrieval (NDTR) adds a project-specific trajectory encoder, expected-transition retrieval, and residual scaling. Contextual NDTR conditions that memory on task family, trajectory phase, and prior tool state. We found no direct published equivalents for these two implementations; they are diagnostics designed to test whether increasingly contextual normality models resolve cross-paradigm failure.

The counterfactual delegation monitor (CDM) is more different still. It aligns an observed history to matched normal reference prefixes and scores the proposed continuation against the closest reference action. This resembles edit-distance sequence alignment and uses counterfactual-like matched information, but it is not a causal estimator or a reproduction of a published method. Because matched references may be unavailable online, CDM should be reported as a post-hoc diagnostic rather than a directly comparable runtime monitor. Likewise, CAAO uses paired post-hoc information and belongs to labeling/oracle analysis, not the online-monitor baseline table.

These classical families expose the paper's central empirical difficulty: novelty is not equivalent to policy violation. A Coding-normal memory can assign high anomaly scores to benign Web transitions because tools, action syntax, and path lengths differ. Conversely, a conservative matched-reference method can suppress both false alarms and detections. A competitive modern sequential baseline remains missing: a DeepLog-style next-transition predictor trained on observable agent events is a submission-priority comparison.

## Domain-General and Invariant Representation Learning

Learning features that transfer across domains is an established objective. DANN uses a gradient-reversal domain classifier so that learned features remain predictive for a labeled source task while becoming difficult to use for domain discrimination [@ganin2016dann]. Deep CORAL aligns second-order feature statistics [@sun2016deepcoral]. Deep Adaptation Networks use maximum mean discrepancy to match mean embeddings in an RKHS [@long2015dan]. IRM seeks a representation for which the same classifier is optimal across training environments [@arjovsky2019irm]. DomainBed showed that evaluation protocol and model selection strongly affect domain-generalization conclusions [@gulrajani2021domainbed]. SelfReg offers a contrastive regularizer that aligns class-specific features across domains [@kim2021selfreg]. Theory also emphasizes a fundamental tradeoff: making a representation invariant can discard information needed by the target response [@zhao2022limits].

PIDR-v1 belongs near this literature, but it does not instantiate these algorithms. It has no adversarial domain classifier, covariance loss, kernel mean discrepancy, or shared supervised classification head. The encoder is a signed-hashed observable trajectory-prefix representation followed by learned diagonal scaling and L2 normalization. Pre-exposure samples aligned by progress and capability encourage reduced paradigm-specific variation. A margin term on matched post-exposure control/treatment transitions is intended to prevent this alignment from erasing intervention-associated differences. Temporal consistency and variance preservation provide local smoothing and collapse control.

Paired intervention supervision also has precedents outside domain generalization. Weakly supervised disentanglement learns from pairs whose generative factors change without full factor annotations [@locatello2020weak]. Brehmer et al. derive causal-identifiability results from paired pre/post samples under unknown interventions [@brehmer2022weakcausal], while interventional causal representation research studies conditions under which latent factors can be recovered from interventional distributions [@ahuja2023interventional; @vonkugelgen2023nonparametric]. These works prevent us from claiming novelty for paired-intervention representation learning. PIDR makes no causal-identifiability claim; condition metadata only constructs training pairs.

The closest missing comparisons are therefore generic, label-light alignment methods. Deep CORAL would test whether second-order alignment alone yields the observed geometry, and MMD would test distribution matching without PIDR's paired separation term. DANN is also relevant but requires a carefully defined task objective. IRM is less natural because DelegationBench lacks a shared semantic class label for benign transitions. Cross-environment benchmarks such as SWE-bench, BrowserGym, and OSWorld establish that Coding, Web, and GUI agents have materially different observation and action interfaces [@jimenez2024swebench; @dechezelles2025browsergym; @xie2024osworld], but they do not by themselves evaluate security-monitor transfer.

**Gap.** Prior work extensively studies agent risk evaluation, indirect prompt injection, sequence anomaly detection, and domain-invariant representation learning. In the primary literature retained by this audit, evaluation of normality-based security monitors across heterogeneous agent paradigms—while preserving variation associated with a controlled delayed intervention—was limited. DelegationBench v1 supplies a paired measurement protocol for that question, and PIDR-v1 is a scoped proof of concept. The strongest current contribution is benchmark and measurement; method-primary claims require direct CORAL/MMD and modern sequential baselines.
